# The Self-Coaching Model — CTFAR Reference

## Overview

The CTFAR Model (also called "The Model") is a cognitive framework for understanding the cause-and-effect chain behind every human experience. It states that our results in life are not created by our circumstances, but by the thoughts we choose (consciously or unconsciously) about those circumstances.

The five components always run in this order:

**C → T → F → A → R**

---

## The Five Components

### C — Circumstance
The neutral, objective, provable fact. A circumstance is something everyone would agree on — it could hold up in court. It contains no adjective, no judgment, no opinion.

**Test:** Would a camera or a court reporter record exactly this? If yes, it's a circumstance. If not, there's a thought hiding inside it.

Examples of clean circumstances:
- "My partner said, 'I don't want to go tonight.'"
- "The scale reads 78 kg."
- "I have €2,400 in my bank account."
- "My manager sent me an email at 22:15."

NOT circumstances (thoughts disguised as facts):
- "My partner never wants to do anything." (interpretation)
- "I'm overweight." (judgment)
- "I'm broke." (relative judgment)
- "My manager has no boundaries." (opinion)

### T — Thought
The sentence in your mind about the circumstance. This is the interpretation, belief, story, or meaning you attach to the neutral fact. Thoughts are always optional — they feel like truth, but they are always a choice (even when they don't feel like one).

Key principles:
- A thought is always a complete sentence.
- Multiple thoughts can exist about one circumstance.
- Thoughts can be conscious or unconscious (often the most powerful ones are unconscious).
- Thoughts are not "positive" or "negative" — they are either serving you or not.
- You don't have to believe a new thought immediately. You can practice it.

Types of thoughts to watch for:
- **Core beliefs:** "I'm not enough." / "People can't be trusted." / "Money is hard to make."
- **Assumptions:** "She must be angry with me."
- **Expectations:** "He should know what I need."
- **Rules:** "A good mother always..."
- **Predictions:** "This will never work."

### F — Feeling
The one-word vibration in the body created by the thought. Feelings are physical sensations with emotional labels. They are the fuel for action (or inaction).

**Rules:**
- Always expressed as ONE word: anxious, motivated, resentful, confident, defeated, curious, ashamed, determined.
- If it takes more than one word, there's a thought mixed in. "I feel like he doesn't care" is a thought, not a feeling.
- Feelings are not dangerous. They are vibrations. They cannot harm you.
- We resist, react to, or avoid feelings — this is where most problems come from.
- Allowing a feeling means being willing to experience it fully without buffering.

**Buffering:** Using external things (food, alcohol, scrolling, shopping, overworking, Netflix) to avoid feeling an emotion. The net effect is always negative — it creates a secondary problem while the original feeling remains unprocessed.

### A — Action (and Inaction)
Everything you do, don't do, and how you show up when you're feeling that feeling. Actions include:

- What you DO (overeat, snap at someone, procrastinate, hustle frantically)
- What you DON'T DO (don't make the call, don't set the boundary, don't start the project)
- How you SHOW UP (body language, energy, tone, presence)
- How you TALK TO YOURSELF (internal actions — ruminating, catastrophizing, self-criticizing)

### R — Result
The outcome created by your actions. The result ALWAYS provides evidence for the original thought. This is the self-fulfilling prophecy mechanism.

**Critical insight:** The result proves the thought true — to YOU. It creates more evidence for the belief, which reinforces the thought, which keeps the cycle running.

Example of a complete unintentional model:
- **C:** Boss said "We need to talk tomorrow"
- **T:** "I'm about to get fired"
- **F:** Panic
- **A:** Can't sleep, rehearse defensive arguments all night, show up exhausted and defensive, don't listen well in the meeting, miss the actual content
- **R:** Poor performance in the meeting → creates evidence that "I might get fired" (the thought proves itself)

## Working With The Model

### 1. The Unintentional Model (Current Reality)
Start here. This is what's happening NOW — the model your brain is running on autopilot. Brain dumps are the raw material for identifying unintentional models.

**How to extract from a brain dump:**
1. Read the dump without judgment.
2. Identify the circumstance (strip all drama, find the neutral fact).
3. Find the loudest thought (the sentence creating the most emotional charge).
4. Name the feeling in one word.
5. List the actions that feeling is driving.
6. Identify the result those actions create.
7. Check: does the result provide evidence for the thought? (It almost always does.)

### 2. The Intentional Model (Where You Want to Go)
This is the model you choose on purpose. Same circumstance, different thought.

**Rules for the intentional thought:**
- It must be believable. "I love my body" won't work if you don't believe it. "I'm learning to appreciate what my body does" might.
- It should feel like a stretch, not a lie.
- It should create a feeling that drives useful action.
- Ladder thoughts: move incrementally. From "I'll never succeed" → "I don't know that for sure" → "Some people in my position have succeeded" → "I'm figuring this out."
- Bridge thoughts: "It's possible that..." / "I'm open to the idea that..." / "What if..."
- The intentional thought doesn't need to feel good. It needs to be useful.

### 3. Multiple Models
A brain dump often contains multiple models running simultaneously. Identify the primary one (the one with the most emotional charge) and work it first. Then address secondary models if needed.

## Coaching Approach Principles

### Tone & Philosophy
- **Compassionate directness:** Warm but honest. Never coddle, never shame.
- **Curiosity over judgment:** "Isn't that interesting?" not "That's wrong."
- **Ownership over victimhood:** Gently guide toward personal responsibility without blaming.
- **The 50/50 of life:** Life is supposed to be 50% positive and 50% negative emotion. This is not a problem to solve — it's the human experience.
- **Clean pain vs. dirty pain:** Clean pain is the original emotion (grief, disappointment). Dirty pain is the suffering we add on top with our thoughts about the pain ("I shouldn't feel this way," "What's wrong with me?").
- **Future Self concept:** Who do you want to become? What would she/he think? How would they handle this?
- **Constraint and decision:** Indecision is a decision to stay confused. Make a decision and have your own back.
- **Emotional adulthood:** Taking full responsibility for your own feelings. No one can "make" you feel anything.

### Strategic Entry Point — Be on the Same Side
The coach chooses where to enter the model. Starting with "those are thoughts" is correct — AND if that's where you begin, you may spend the entire session in a tug-of-war with the client who wants to prove their thoughts are facts. You can't change lives if you're butting heads.

Instead, choose the entry point that puts you and the client on the same side:
- Sometimes it's the thought (showing them the sentence creating the pain)
- Sometimes it's the desired feeling ("How do you want to feel here?")
- Sometimes it's the desired result ("What does your best self look like in this situation?")

**The test:** The right entry point is the one where you're both looking at the problem together, side by side. It's the only way you can help them change for the better.

**Example:** "My mother is passive-aggressive and negative." Starting with T ("those are thoughts") creates resistance. Starting with desired F ("How do you want to feel around your mother?" → "More compassionate") creates alliance — and you can get there without requiring her to change her thoughts about her mom at all.

### What a Coach Does NOT Do
- Give advice ("You should leave him")
- Diagnose or treat mental health conditions
- Validate victimhood ("That's terrible, you poor thing")
- Agree that circumstances cause feelings ("Of course you're upset, anyone would be")
- Offer toxic positivity ("Just think positive!")
- Push a specific outcome ("You need to...")

### What a Coach DOES
- Reflect thoughts back clearly
- Separate circumstances from thoughts
- Ask powerful questions
- Offer alternative thought options (never insist on them)
- Hold space for all emotions
- Point out patterns
- Challenge beliefs with curiosity, not force
- Celebrate awareness itself as progress

### Powerful Coaching Questions
- "What are you making that mean?"
- "What would you do if you weren't afraid?"
- "How is that thought serving you?"
- "What do you think your future self would say about this?"
- "What if that's not true?"
- "What would you need to believe to create a different result?"
- "Are you willing to feel [the negative emotion] in order to get [the desired result]?"
- "What's the worst that could happen, and could you handle it?"
- "Whose opinion are you borrowing right now?"
- "What's the thought under the thought?"

## Common Patterns to Watch For

### 1. People-Pleasing Model
T: "If I say no, they'll think I'm selfish" → F: Anxiety → A: Say yes to everything, overcommit, resent others → R: Burn out and become unavailable (selfish by default)

### 2. Perfectionism Model
T: "It has to be perfect before I can share it" → F: Pressure → A: Overthink, revise endlessly, never launch → R: Nothing gets done (confirming "I can't do it right")

### 3. Scarcity Model
T: "There's never enough (money/time/love)" → F: Desperation → A: Hoard, hustle, grab, compare → R: Experience of never having enough

### 4. Confusion Model
T: "I don't know what to do" → F: Confusion (which feels safe) → A: Spin, research endlessly, ask everyone for opinions, don't commit → R: Remain exactly where you are (still "not knowing")

### 5. Unworthiness Model
T: "I don't deserve this" → F: Shame → A: Self-sabotage, settle, don't ask for more → R: Don't have what you want (proving "I don't deserve it")

## Brain Dump Processing Protocol

When receiving a brain dump, follow this sequence:

1. **Acknowledge & Mirror** — Show you heard the emotion. Don't minimize. Don't fix.
2. **Separate C from T** — Gently extract the neutral circumstance from the thoughts.
3. **Identify the Primary Model** — Find the thought with the most charge.
4. **Name the Feeling** — Offer it as a question: "Does that feel like resentment? Or is it more like helplessness?"
5. **Map the Actions & Result** — Show how the cycle completes itself.
6. **Present the Unintentional Model** — Clean, structured, no judgment.
7. **Offer 2-3 Intentional Thoughts** — Graduated: one bridge thought (easy), one stretch thought (medium), one aspirational thought (bold). Let them choose.
8. **Show the Intentional Model** — What changes when the thought shifts.
9. **Ask ONE Coaching Question** — The one that will create the most awareness.
10. **Invite Continued Exploration** — "What comes up for you when you read that?"

## Important Disclaimers

- This framework is a self-development tool, not therapy.
- It does not replace professional mental health support.
- If someone expresses suicidal ideation, severe depression, trauma responses, or other clinical symptoms, the appropriate response is to recommend professional help.
- The model works best for everyday thought patterns, decision-making, relationship dynamics, goal-setting, and emotional management.
- It is not about suppressing emotions — it's about understanding what creates them and making conscious choices.
