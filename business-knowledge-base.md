## PART 1: CORE BUSINESS FRAMEWORKS

### The Grand Slam Offer (Hormozi)
An offer so good people feel stupid saying no. Differentiated, incomparable, value-driven (not price-driven). Makes it almost impossible to lose because it helps get more customers, get them to pay more, and buy more often.

**The Value Equation:**
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

To increase value: increase dream outcome, increase perceived likelihood, decrease time delay, decrease effort required.

**Building a Grand Slam Offer:**
1. Identify dream outcome (what they actually want)
2. List all problems/obstacles between them and the dream outcome
3. For each problem, create a solution
4. Trim solutions that don't move the needle; stack the rest
5. Enhance with: Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees, Naming

**Finding the Right Market (Starving Crowd):**
4 indicators: (1) Massive pain, (2) Purchasing power, (3) Easy to target, (4) Growing market.

### The Core Four — Lead Generation (Hormozi)
Only 4 ways to get leads, combining two axes: warm/cold audience × one-to-one/one-to-many.

1. **Warm Outreach** (1-to-1, warm) — reach out personally to people who know you
2. **Post Free Content** (1-to-many, warm) — create content that attracts and educates
3. **Cold Outreach** (1-to-1, cold) — reach strangers directly
4. **Run Paid Ads** (1-to-many, cold) — pay to reach strangers at scale

**More Better New:** Once one channel works, do MORE of it, make it BETTER, then add a NEW channel. Don't scatter.

**Lead Getters (Level 2):** Get OTHER people to run the Core Four for you: Customer Referrals, Employees, Agencies, Affiliates/Partners.

### Lead Magnets (Hormozi)
A complete solution to a narrow problem. Once solved, it reveals another problem solved by your core offer.

7 steps: (1) Identify target problem, (2) Figure out how to solve it, (3) Determine delivery method, (4) Name it, (5) Make it easy to consume, (6) Make it so good they feel stupid saying no, (7) Add a CTA that bridges to your core offer.

### The Value Ladder (Brunson — DotCom Secrets)
Ascension model: free → low-ticket → mid-ticket → high-ticket. Each step provides more value at a higher price. Don't try to sell your most expensive thing first. Lead with value, build trust, then offer the next level.

### Hook, Story, Offer (Brunson — Traffic Secrets)
Every piece of content/marketing has 3 elements:
- **Hook**: grabs attention (headline, image, first line)
- **Story**: builds connection and perceived value (increases desire, breaks false beliefs)
- **Offer**: what they get and what they do next (CTA)

### The Dream 100 (Brunson)
Identify the 100 people/platforms who already have your ideal audience. Infiltrate by providing value, building relationships, earning access. Work your way in or buy your way in.

### The Attractive Character (Brunson)
Your brand persona that connects with your audience through: backstory, parables, character flaws, and polarity. Communicate through Soap Opera Sequences (email/story arcs) and Seinfeld Sequences (daily interesting content).

### The SB7 Framework (Donald Miller — StoryBrand)
Your customer is the hero, your brand is the guide.

1. **A character** (your customer) has a problem
2. **And meets a guide** (your brand) who shows empathy and authority
3. **Who gives them a plan** (clear steps)
4. **And calls them to action** (direct CTA)
5. **That helps them avoid failure** (stakes)
6. **And ends in success** (transformation)

**The Grunt Test:** Can someone look at your website and within 5 seconds answer: What do you offer? How will it make my life better? What do I do to buy it?

**One-liner formula:** [Problem] + [Solution] + [Result]

### Permission Marketing (Seth Godin)
Stop interrupting strangers. Instead, earn the right to deliver anticipated, personal, relevant messages to people who want to get them.

**The ladder:** Strangers → Friends → Customers. Build permission assets (email lists, followers who opted in). Permission is like dating — you don't propose on the first date.

### This Is Marketing (Seth Godin)
- Marketing is the generous act of helping someone solve a problem
- Find the smallest viable market — the minimum number of people you need to make it worth it
- Create tension and then relieve it — the status quo is the enemy
- "People like us do things like this" — tap into identity and belonging
- Enrollment, not coercion — help people become who they want to be
- You can't be seen until you learn to see (empathy first)

### The Purple Cow (Seth Godin)
In a crowded marketplace, the only way to win is to be REMARKABLE — worth talking about. Safe is risky. Boring is invisible.

- The TV-industrial complex is dead. Mass marketing to average people with average products no longer works.
- Design your product/service to be remarkable from the start — don't add marketing to a boring product.
- Find the "sneezers" (the people who spread ideas) and the "otaku" (the obsessives in your niche).
- Go to the edges. The fringes are where the remarkable stuff lives.

### Perennial Seller (Ryan Holiday)
Create work that lasts. Not viral, not trendy — enduring.

- **The Work:** Start by making something remarkable. Not marketing-first, creation-first. Ask: "Will this be relevant in 10 years?"
- **Positioning:** A perennial seller solves a timeless problem for a timeless audience.
- **Marketing:** Word of mouth is the only sustainable strategy. Earn it by being so good people can't NOT talk about you.
- **Platform:** Build your own audience (email list, community). Don't depend on platforms you don't own.
- One free customer at a time. Compound over years.

### Unreasonable Hospitality (Will Guidara)
- "Service is black and white; hospitality is color."
- Service = competence + efficiency. Hospitality = making people FEEL seen, valued, and welcome.
- **One-size-fits-one**: Customize the experience. Pay attention to what each person needs.
- **The 95/5 rule**: Manage 95% of operations with discipline and systems. Use the remaining 5% for unreasonable, creative, memorable gestures.
- Excellence is a prerequisite. Hospitality is the differentiator.
- How you treat your team IS how they'll treat your customers.

### Profit First (Mike Michalowicz)
Flip the formula: Sales - Profit = Expenses (not Sales - Expenses = Profit).

**The Small Plates system:**
1. Set up 4 bank accounts: Profit, Owner's Pay, Tax, Operating Expenses
2. Every time money comes in, allocate immediately by percentage
3. Use the Instant Assessment to see where you really are vs where you should be
4. Enforce a rhythm: pay bills on the 10th and 25th only
5. Use a "temptation-free" bank for Profit and Tax (hard to access)

**Target Allocation Percentages (TAPs):** Start where you are, move 1-2% closer each quarter toward: 5% Profit, 50% Owner's Pay, 15% Tax, 30% OpEx (varies by revenue level).

### Who Not How (Dan Sullivan)
When you have a goal, don't ask "How do I do this?" Ask "WHO can do this for me?"

- **How-thinking** = slow, exhausting, limits you to your own capabilities
- **Who-thinking** = fast, leveraged, expands beyond your abilities
- Every "How" is actually a "Who" waiting to be found
- Freedom of Time, Freedom of Money, Freedom of Relationship, Freedom of Purpose — all come from finding the right Whos
- Your Unique Ability is the only thing YOU should be doing. Everything else gets a Who.
- Being a good Who for others = show up with commitment, deliver results, be proactive

## PART 2: ENTREPRENEUR MINDSET (Thought Work Meets Business)

### Brain Management for Entrepreneurs (Brooke Castillo)
- Your overarching compelling "why" is your fuel through obstacles
- Separate DESIRE from COMMITMENT — desire fluctuates, commitment doesn't
- Math problems vs Thought problems: Target Market, Traffic, Opt-in, Offer, Delivery are math. Time, Overwhelm, Confusion, Procrastination, Doubt, Fear are thought problems. Solve each with the right tool.

### Overcoming Failure
- Fear of failure is the main reason people don't start
- Define YOUR definition of failure
- List common excuses you use to avoid failing
- Make a failure plan: list every possible failure and plan your response
- Practice a believable thought that creates courage on purpose
- Failure isn't the opposite of success — it's the path to success

### The No Matter What Factor
- Write your financial goal, then break it down by quarter
- Write your plan. Make the math exact.
- If it doesn't work: The "No Matter What" Factor + The "Answer to How is Yes" Factor + The "Learn to Fail, Rinse, Repeat" Factor

### Entrepreneur vs Technician
- Two roles: entrepreneur (vision, money, strategy) and technician (the craft, delivery)
- Most solopreneurs are trapped in technician mode
- Schedule distinct time for each role
- Delegate technician tasks as soon as possible (Who Not How)

### Narrowing Your Niche (LCS)
- Does your problem fit Wealth, Health, or Relationships?
- Narrow to a submarket with at least 2 defining words
- What would someone Google to find you?
- What is your solution and why is it compelling?

### Give Results Ahead of Time (LCS)
- Show people you can help them by actually helping them for free
- This IS marketing — when you provide results first, there's no need for hard sales
- Brainstorm 10 ways to give results ahead of time, closely related to your paid offer

### Make Irresistible Offers (LCS)
- Your offer = the value you provide in exchange for money
- Make it irresistible: lower risk, increase perceived value, add guarantees

### Cash Flow and Profit (LCS)
- Revenue - Expenses = Profit. You want more coming in than going out.
- Think in terms of assets (things that produce revenue) vs liabilities (things that cost money)
- Be proactive with income, not reactive
- Project 3-year revenue, profit, expenses

### Business Model Planning (LCS)
- How will you make money? One-on-one? Group? Products? Subscriptions?
- Annual target ÷ 4 = quarterly target
- Experiment with math: how many clients × what price = your number

### 10-Year Plan (LCS)
- Work backwards: 10 years → 5 years → 1 year
- Three columns: Money, Customers, Assets
- Assets = things you create that are worth something (books, programs, courses, email lists)

### Narrative Systems (LCS)
- Document every component of your business the client experiences
- Create before/during/after for each component
- Identify who does each step
- This IS your operations manual

### 50 Thoughts Successful People Think (Jody Moore)
Key business-relevant ones:
- Everything is figure-out-able
- B- work gets the job done / Done is better than perfect
- The only way I fail is if I quit
- Small, consistent actions will get me there
- I am willing to be not good at this for as long as necessary
- I'm not for everyone — it's ok for people to be wrong about me
- Other people's success is proof of what's possible
- Nobody else needs to believe in me — that's my job
- I can do this my way
- My people need me / My people can't afford not to buy this

### Business-Minded Framework (Jody Moore)
6-step cycle: Set a 30-60 day goal → Take an educated guess → Output at B- level → Invest time or money → Observe and learn → Make it better. Repeat.

### The Machine of Your Business (Jody Moore)
3 buckets: Lead Bucket → Fan Bucket → Customer Bucket. Each needs a plan for: what draws people in, how you nurture them, what you offer, what happens after they buy (or don't).

## PART 3: APPLIED BY PROJECT TYPE

### For SEO / Website Building
- **StoryBrand** the client's website: hero (their customer), guide (them), plan, CTA
- **Grunt Test** every website: what, how it helps, what to do next — in 5 seconds
- **Dream 100**: identify the 100 potential clients who need your services
- **Give Results Ahead of Time**: free site audit, free SEO report, case study
- **Core Four**: warm outreach to network, free content showing expertise, cold outreach to businesses with bad websites, targeted ads
- **Value Equation**: increase dream outcome (more traffic, more sales), decrease time/effort (you do it for them)

### For Book Publishing (Art / Photography / Niche)
- **Perennial Seller**: make the book so good it sells for decades, not weeks
- **Purple Cow**: the book itself must be remarkable — design, concept, angle
- **Permission Marketing**: build an audience BEFORE the book launches (email list, social following of people who care about this niche)
- **Value Ladder**: free content → lead magnet (excerpt, behind-the-scenes) → book → limited edition → workshops/prints
- **Smallest Viable Market**: find the obsessives in your niche (the "otaku"), serve them deeply

### For B&B (Hospitality)
- **Unreasonable Hospitality**: the 95/5 rule — run operations with excellence, then add 5% "magic moments" that guests will tell everyone about
- **StoryBrand the website**: the guest is the hero seeking rest/adventure/escape, you are the guide
- **One-size-fits-one**: personalize the experience for each guest
- **Permission Marketing**: capture emails, nurture with beautiful seasonal content, earn repeat visits
- **Dream 100**: partner with local attractions, wedding planners, travel bloggers, photographers
- **Profit First**: separate accounts from day one — hospitality businesses bleed cash if undisciplined

## PART 4: BUSINESS COACHING QUESTIONS

### Offer & Positioning
- What is the dream outcome your customer wants? Be specific.
- What are ALL the obstacles between them and that dream outcome? List everything.
- For each obstacle, what solution could you provide?
- What would make your offer so good people feel stupid saying no?
- What risk are you asking the customer to take? How can you reduce it (guarantee, trial)?
- Is your market in massive pain? Do they have purchasing power? Are they easy to find? Is the market growing?
- What would someone Google at 2am to find you?

### Pricing & Value
- Are you charging based on cost or value? What would a value-based price look like?
- How can you increase the dream outcome of your offer?
- How can you increase the perceived likelihood your customer will succeed?
- How can you decrease the time it takes to get the result?
- How can you decrease the effort required from the customer?

### Lead Generation
- Which of the Core Four are you currently doing? Which are you avoiding? Why?
- What is your lead magnet? Does it solve a narrow problem that reveals a bigger one you solve for pay?
- Who are your Dream 100 — the people/platforms who already have your audience?
- Are you giving results ahead of time? In what ways?
- What would "More Better New" look like for your current lead gen?

### Marketing & Messaging
- Can someone look at your website and in 5 seconds know: what you offer, how it makes their life better, what to do next?
- What is your one-liner? [Problem] + [Solution] + [Result]
- Who is the hero of your marketing — you or your customer?
- What story are you telling? What transformation does it promise?
- Are you being remarkable, or are you being safe? Where could you take a risk?

### Money & Operations
- Do you know your numbers? Revenue, expenses, profit — right now, today?
- Run the Instant Assessment: what percentage goes to profit? Owner's pay? Tax? OpEx?
- What can you DELETE from your workload? DELEGATE? DELIGHT in?
- How much time are you spending as entrepreneur vs technician? What's the ideal split?
- What is the ONE Who you need to find to unlock your next level?

### Mindset & Resistance
- What is your "no matter what" reason for building this business?
- What would you do if you knew you couldn't fail?
- Where are you buffering with confusion instead of taking action?
- What is the thought problem disguised as a math problem?
- What would your future self — the one who's already built this — tell you right now?
- Are you doing B- work and shipping, or perfecting and hiding?
- What are you tolerating in your business that your future self wouldn't accept?

### Growth & Scale
- What is your 10-year vision? Work backwards to 5 years, then 1 year.
- What are the assets you're building? (Not just revenue — what compounds?)
- What would it look like to double your business? What's the one lever?
- Where are you doing "How" when you should be finding a "Who"?
- What is your Unique Ability — the thing only YOU should be doing?

### For B&B Specifically
- What is the experience you want guests to remember and tell others about?
- What's your "5% unreasonable gesture" — the thing that surprises and delights?
- How are you making each guest feel individually seen?
- What partnerships could bring you guests without advertising?

### For Publishing Specifically
- Is this book designed to last 10 years, or is it chasing a trend?
- Who are the obsessives in this niche? Where do they gather?
- What is remarkable about this book that makes it worth talking about?
- What's your Value Ladder beyond the book itself?
- Are you building an audience before you publish, or hoping the book finds them?

### For SEO/Website Specifically
- What results can you show before the client pays?
- How are you positioning yourself differently from every other SEO/web agency?
- What's your guarantee or risk reversal?
- Are you speaking your client's language or your technical language?
